Exploratory data analysis of the Argentine labor market using microdata from INDEC's **Encuesta Permanente de Hogares (EPH)** — the country's official household survey, covering 31 urban agglomerations and approximately 29 million people.

---

## 🎯 Objective

Translate raw government microdata into clear, accessible insights about employment, informality, gender wage gaps, and youth unemployment in Argentina — making official statistics understandable for non-technical audiences.

---

## 🔍 Analyses Performed

| # | Analysis | Key Finding |
|---|----------|-------------|
| 1 | General employment situation | Estimated unemployment rate: **7.5%** |
| 2 | Unemployment by gender | Higher absolute unemployment among men |
| 3 | Unemployment by region | **Gran Buenos Aires** holds the highest rate nationally |
| 4 | Gender wage gap | Men earn **~42% more** than women on average |
| 5 | Unemployment by education level | Mid-level education shows highest vulnerability |
| 6 | Youth unemployment (15–24) | **20.3%** — nearly 3× the national average |
| 7 | Labor informality | ~**40%** of salaried workers have no pension contributions |

---

## 💡 Key Insights

- **Youth unemployment at 20.3%** is one of the most critical structural problems in the Argentine labor market, driven by barriers to formal entry and skill mismatches.
- **The gender wage gap (~42%)** persists across all sectors, with women also disproportionately affected by informality.
- **Gran Buenos Aires** concentrates the highest unemployment, linked to the decline of industrial activity over the past two years.
- **40% informality** remains high despite recent labor reforms, with women facing higher relative exposure.
